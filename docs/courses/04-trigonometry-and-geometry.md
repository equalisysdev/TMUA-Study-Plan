# Course 4: Trigonometry and Geometry

A lesson-first course for exact trigonometry, coordinate geometry, circles, and structured diagram reasoning.

## How to use this course

Draw a diagram first. Keep exact values until the final line and check whether the answer fits the geometry.

## Prerequisites

You should know basic angle measure, right-triangle ratios, coordinates, and algebraic rearrangement.

## Learning outcomes

- use exact values and the unit circle
- solve trigonometric equations on a stated interval
- manipulate identities safely
- use line, circle, distance, midpoint, and angle formulas
- select the sine rule, cosine rule, or area formula appropriately

## Lesson 1: Exact values and the unit circle

The coordinates of a point on the unit circle are `(cos x, sin x)`. Memorise the exact values for `0`, `pi/6`, `pi/4`, `pi/3`, and `pi/2`, then use symmetry to handle other quadrants.

**Worked example:** `sin(x)=1/2` on `[0,2pi]` gives `x=pi/6` or `x=5pi/6`.

## Lesson 2: Identities and equations

The central identity is `sin^2(x)+cos^2(x)=1`. Dividing by `cos^2(x)` gives `1+tan^2(x)=sec^2(x)` where defined. Simplify one side at a time and state any values excluded by division.

**Worked example:** `sin(2x)=0` means `2x=npi`. On `[0,pi]`, the solutions are `x=0`, `pi/2`, and `pi`.

## Lesson 3: Coordinates and circles

For points `(x1,y1)` and `(x2,y2)`:

- gradient: `(y2-y1)/(x2-x1)`
- midpoint: `((x1+x2)/2,(y1+y2)/2)`
- distance squared: `(x2-x1)^2+(y2-y1)^2`

A circle with centre `(a,b)` and radius `r` has equation `(x-a)^2+(y-b)^2=r^2`.

**Worked example:** `x^2+y^2-6x+4y-3=0` becomes `(x-3)^2+(y+2)^2=16`, so the centre is `(3,-2)` and radius is `4`.

## Lesson 4: Triangles and area

Use `area=1/2 ab sin(C)` when two sides and the included angle are known. Use the cosine rule for a missing side or angle when three sides or two sides plus an included angle are available.

**Worked example:** With sides `5` and `7` enclosing `60` degrees, the third side `c` satisfies `c^2=5^2+7^2-2(5)(7)cos(60)`, so `c=sqrt(39)`.

## Common errors

- giving decimal answers when an exact value is available
- missing the second solution in a trigonometric interval
- using the cosine rule with the wrong included angle
- forgetting that a line gradient is undefined for a vertical line
- expanding a circle without completing the square correctly

## Checkpoint

Solve a trigonometric equation on a full interval, then verify every candidate in the original equation.
