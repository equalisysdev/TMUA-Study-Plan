# Proof, Logic, and Combinatorics Drills

## Learning objectives
- structure clear mathematical arguments
- work with induction and contradiction logically
- count efficiently using combinations and permutations
- solve probability and combinatorics questions without overcounting

## Exercise Set A: Proof and logic

### 1. Prove that the sum of two even numbers is even.

### 2. Prove by induction that
$$
1+2+$cdots+n = \$frac{n(n+1)}{2}
$$

### 3. Show that if $n$ is odd, then $n^2$ is odd.

### 4. Prove that if $a>b$ and $c>0$, then $ac>bc$.

### 5. A number is divisible by 3 if the sum of its digits is divisible by 3. Explain why this is true for a two-digit number $10a+b$.

## Exercise Set B: Counting and probability

### 6. In how many ways can 5 students be arranged in a line?

### 7. How many ways can a committee of 3 be chosen from 8 people?

### 8. A bag has 4 red and 3 blue balls. Find the probability of drawing a red ball.

### 9. Two fair dice are rolled. Find the probability that the total is 7.

### 10. How many 4-digit numbers can be formed using digits 1,2,3,4 if no repetition is allowed?

## Exercise Set C: Mixed reasoning

### 11. A test has 10 questions. In how many ways can a student choose 6 to answer?

### 12. What is the coefficient of $x^3$ in $(1+x)^5$?

### 13. If a coin is tossed 3 times, what is the probability of exactly two heads?

### 14. A class has 20 students. How many ways can a captain and vice-captain be chosen?

### 15. A box contains 2 green, 3 yellow, and 5 blue balls. What is the probability of drawing a yellow or blue ball?

## Technique focus
- Use direct proof and contradiction only where natural
- For induction, clearly state base case and inductive step
- For combinatorics, distinguish arrangement from selection
- For probability, define total outcomes before counting favourable ones

## Answer notes
- Question 6: permutations
- Question 7: combinations
- Question 8: favourable over total outcomes
- Question 9: count favourable sums
- Question 10: use permutation logic
- Question 12: binomial coefficient
- Questions 13–15: practise exact counting and inclusion logic
