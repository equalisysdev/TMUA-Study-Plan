# TMUA Study Programme

## Overview
This programme is built for a 16-week preparation period ending on January 4th. It balances concept mastery, speed, and exam-style conditioning.

## Training philosophy
A strong TMUA score comes from three habits:
- accurate mathematical technique
- quick recognition of standard patterns
- disciplined decision-making under time pressure

The best preparation is therefore not only pure revision; it is a mixture of:
- learning the concept
- practising questions in a controlled way
- doing mixed timed sets
- reviewing errors and repeating weak patterns

## Stage goals

### Stage 1: Diagnostic and foundation (Weeks 1–2)
Build a baseline and repair weak fundamentals.

Focus:
- algebraic manipulation
- expanding and factorising
- solving equations and inequalities
- function notation and graph transformations
- basic sequences and series

Target:
- identify weak topics early
- complete 2–3 practice sets with review after every one

### Stage 2: Core mathematics and technique (Weeks 3–7)
Strengthen the main TMUA content areas.

Focus:
- quadratic and polynomial work
- functions, graphs, and transformations
- calculus basics and applications
- trigonometry and geometry
- proof and algebraic reasoning

Target:
- dependable technique across all common question types
- speed improvement without sacrificing accuracy

### Stage 3: Mixed problem-solving and timed practice (Weeks 8–12)
Convert routine skill into exam logic.

Focus:
- mixed question sets
- time management
- eliminating distractors
- selecting efficient methods

Target:
- solve most questions in a controlled way under time pressure
- reduce slow, error-prone methods

### Stage 4: Final preparation and exam simulation (Weeks 13–16)
Use the last weeks to sharpen execution and confidence.

Focus:
- full-length timed papers
- targeted repair of mistakes
- formula recall and quick method selection
- exam-day calm and pacing

Target:
- strong and consistent accuracy in timed conditions
- a final exam performance that matches your best practice

## Weekly structure
Each week should include:
- 3 concept-learning sessions
- 2 technique drill sessions
- 1 mixed review session
- 1 timed paper or timed batch
- 1 error-recovery session

### Recommended weekly timetable
- Monday: algebra / function skill work
- Tuesday: calculus or geometry skill work
- Wednesday: mixed drill set
- Thursday: proof / combinatorics / reasoning
- Friday: timed batch or exam-style section
- Saturday: longer timed paper or review session
- Sunday: reset and weak-area catch-up

## Performance targets
Aim for steadily improving accuracy and pacing:
- Weeks 1–2: identify patterns and weak areas
- Weeks 3–7: build reliable method accuracy
- Weeks 8–12: improve speed under timed conditions
- Weeks 13–16: consolidate and simulate exam performance

## Core exam techniques
- Read the question fully before calculating
- Eliminate impossible options early
- Prefer cleaner algebraic methods over brutal expansion
- Check special cases and domain restrictions
- Do not waste time on one hard question; move on and return later
- Keep an error \\log and review it every week

## Revision method
After every timed set:
1. Mark correct, incorrect, and omitted questions
2. Explain the reason for each mistake
3. Redo the missed question without notes
4. Note the recurring pattern in the error \\log

## Key principle
The strongest technique is not raw memorisation but flexible problem solving under pressure. TMUA rewards mathematical fluency, clarity of method, and control under time limits.
