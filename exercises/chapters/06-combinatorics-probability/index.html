Chapter 6: Combinatorics and Probability

State whether order matters before choosing a counting method.

Foundation

  1. How many ways can 5 books be arranged on a shelf?
  2. How many committees of 3 can be chosen from 8 people?
  3. How many 3-digit numbers use distinct digits from 1,2,3,4,5?
  4. How many outcomes are possible when two dice are rolled?
  5. Find the probability of rolling a 6 on a fair die.
  6. Find the probability of exactly two heads in three fair coin tosses.
  7. Find the probability of a total of 7 with two fair dice.
  8. How many subsets does a set of 5 elements have?
  9. Find the coefficient of $x^2$ in $(1+x)^5$.
  10. In how many ways can a captain and vice-captain be chosen from 10 people?

Core technique

  1. How many arrangements of the letters in MATH are possible?
  2. How many arrangements of A,B,C,D use A before B?
  3. How many 4-digit even numbers can be made from 1,2,3,4,5 without repetition?
  4. Find the probability of drawing two red cards from a standard deck without replacement.
  5. A bag has 4 red and 6 blue balls. Find the probability of two balls of different colours without replacement.
  6. A fair die is rolled twice. Find the probability that the second score exceeds the first.
  7. Find the expected score of a fair six-sided die.
  8. Find the coefficient of $x^3$ in $(2+x)^5$.
  9. How many solutions in nonnegative integers satisfy $a+b+c=7$?
  10. How many paths move from (0,0) to (3,2) using only right and up steps?

TMUA challenge

  1. How many arrangements of the letters in BANANA are distinct?
  2. How many committees of 4 from 10 include two specified people and exclude a third?
  3. A family has 4 children. Find the probability of exactly two girls, assuming equal independent probabilities.
  4. Three balls are drawn from 5 red and 4 blue. Find the probability of exactly two red balls.
  5. Two dice are rolled. Find the probability that the product is a multiple of 6.
  6. Find the expected number of heads in 8 fair coin tosses.
  7. How many positive integer solutions satisfy $x+y+z=10$?
  8. In how many ways can 6 people sit around a circular table?
  9. A test has 8 questions, each with 4 choices. How many answer patterns contain exactly two answers of choice A?
  10. A random permutation of 1,2,3,4,5 is selected. Find the probability that no number is in its original position.
