Chapter 3: Calculus and Change

Use exact values. Sketch curves and derivative sign charts where they clarify the argument.

Foundation

  1. Differentiate x^5-3x^2+7.
  2. Differentiate 4x^3-1/x.
  3. Differentiate (x+1)(x^2-2).
  4. Differentiate x^2 e^x.
  5. Find the gradient of y=x^3-4x at x=2.
  6. Find the stationary points of y=x^3-3x.
  7. Integrate 6x^2-4x+1.
  8. Integrate 1/x.
  9. Evaluate integral_0^2 (3x^2+1) dx.
  10. Find the tangent to y=x^2+3x at x=1.

Core technique

  1. Find and classify the stationary points of x^3-6x^2+9x+2.
  2. Find where f(x)=x^3-3x^2-9x is increasing.
  3. Find the area between y=x and y=x^2 from 0 to 1.
  4. Evaluate integral_1^e (1/x) dx.
  5. Find y if dy/dx=4x-3 and y=2 when x=1.
  6. Differentiate (2x+1)^5.
  7. Differentiate ln(x^2+1).
  8. Find the normal to y=x^2 at (1,1).
  9. Find the x-coordinate of the maximum of x(10-x).
  10. A particle has s(t)=t^3-6t^2+9t; find when it is at rest.

TMUA challenge

  1. Find the area enclosed by y=4-x^2 and the x-axis.
  2. Find the minimum of x+4/x for x>0 using calculus.
  3. A curve has derivative 3x^2-12x+9. Find intervals of increase and decrease.
  4. Find all tangents to y=x^2 passing through (0,-1).
  5. Evaluate integral_0^1 x ln(x) dx by integration by parts.
  6. Find the stationary points of y=x^4-4x^2 and classify them.
  7. A rectangle under y=12-x^2 has one side on the x-axis. Find its maximum area.
  8. Find the number of roots of x^3-3x+1=0 using the derivative and graph shape.
  9. If f'(x)=2x/(x^2+1) and f(0)=4, find f(x).
  10. Prove that x^2+1/x^2>=2 for positive x, then identify when equality occurs.
