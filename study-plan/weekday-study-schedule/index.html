Weekday Study Schedule Based on Your Real Availability

Weekly working rule

Your realistic study windows are: - a 1-hour lunch break - a 30-minute ride home - weekend focused blocks

This plan is built to stay productive without requiring a longer lunch block.

Standard weekday structure

Lunch block (1 hour)

Use this as the main study block and split it as follows:

  1. 15 minutes: quick warm-up and formula recall
  2. 25 minutes: concept work or technique learning
  3. 20 minutes: short exercise set

This should be your highest-quality study session of the day.

Ride home block (30 minutes)

Use this for lighter revision only: - formula recall - error-\log review - 5–8 quick questions - short topic recap

This is not the time for difficult multi-step reasoning.

Example weekday timetable

Monday

  • Lunch: Algebra and functions
  • Ride home: 5 quick function questions + error \log

Tuesday

  • Lunch: Calculus core concepts
  • Ride home: differentiate/integrate quick recall

Wednesday

  • Lunch: Trigonometry and geometry
  • Ride home: trig identities and diagram recap

Thursday

  • Lunch: Proof, logic, and combinatorics
  • Ride home: counting and probability flash review

Friday

  • Lunch: Mixed timed set or mock section
  • Ride home: mark answers and note weak topics

Weekly target

A realistic weekly target with this timetable is: - 5 lunch blocks of 1 hour = 5 hours - 5 commute blocks of 30 minutes = 2.5 hours - 1 weekend study block of around 90–120 minutes = 1.5–2 hours - total: approximately 9–10 hours per week

This is enough for consistent progress if you keep to it.

Weekend structure

Saturday

  • 90 minutes: timed paper or hard mixed set
  • 45 minutes: error correction and note review

Sunday

  • 60 minutes: weak-topic recovery
  • 30 minutes: confidence-building questions
  • 20 minutes: planning for the next week

Best use of your lunch break

With only 1 hour, aim for: - 10–15 minutes recall warm-up - 25–30 minutes concept review - 15–20 minutes practice - 5 minutes check mistakes

This keeps the session sharp and manageable.

Weekly goal by phase

  • Foundation weeks: focus on technique and accuracy
  • Mid-phase: mixed practice and pacing
  • Final 4 weeks: timed sections and mock exams

Minimal effective version

If you are tired one day, keep the minimum standard: - 1 lunch block of 45–60 minutes - 1 ride-home review of 20–30 minutes - 1 short error \log

Consistency matters more than intensity.
